## Bout summary
This was a busy, well-populated bout: 14 human-confirmed touches across 180 seconds, with in-play action making up about 57 percent of the recording. The fencers spent most of their time in the tactically live ranges, with 45 percent of the bout at lunge distance and another 35 percent at advance-lunge range. True infighting was rare (9.4 percent) and only about 11 percent of the time was spent out of distance, so both fencers were generally engaged and close enough for exchanges to develop. Both fencers pushed more ground than they gave back, but the net-displacement figures that would tell us who pressed harder are flagged as unreliable on this recording, so I will not draw a firm conclusion about who dominated the ground.

## Observed tendencies
- Touches were closely spaced, with 14 confirmed over 180 seconds, including several tight clusters (35, 43, 48 seconds and 106, 112 seconds), suggesting phases where exchanges came in quick succession rather than being spread evenly.
- The bout lived at lunge distance: 45.2 percent of time in the 1.5 to 2.6 m band, the range where a lunge can actually score, indicating both fencers were repeatedly willing to work at scoring range.
- A further 34.7 percent was spent at advance-lunge range (2.6 to 3.5 m), so a large share of the action involved preparation and step-in play rather than direct arrival.
- Close infighting was uncommon at 9.4 percent, and the minimum recorded distance of 0.04 m suggests only brief moments of very tight contact rather than sustained body-to-body work.
- The final two touches came late (172 and 179 seconds), so the bout stayed active right to the end of the recording rather than tailing off.

## Suggestions to explore
- Review the clustered touch sequences (around 43 to 48 seconds and 106 to 112 seconds) on video to see what pattern was repeating and whether one fencer was recovering position quickly or being caught out.
- Look at how the advance-lunge range (the 35 percent of time in the 2.6 to 3.5 m band) was being resolved: was the step-in creating openings, or was it mostly preparation that reset without an attempt?
- Since infighting was rare, the coach could check whether the fencers are comfortable when distance does collapse below 1.5 m, or whether they tend to break off rather than fight it out.

## Data caveats
These metrics come from a computer-vision prototype, so all distances are estimates normalised by fencer height rather than precise measurements. The touch count and timing are human-confirmed and reliable, but the data does not say who scored, what score resulted, or what action produced any touch, so none of that should be inferred. Importantly, the net_forward_m and push_share_pct figures are flagged as unreliable on this recording due to uncorrected camera motion, so they must not be read as evidence of aggression or territorial gain; any comment above on pressing the ground is deliberately left open for that reason.
