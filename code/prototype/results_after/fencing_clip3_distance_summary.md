## Bout summary
This was a busy, evenly contested bout. Fourteen touches were confirmed across three minutes, a rate of about 4.67 per minute, with roughly 57 per cent of the recording spent in live play. The fencers lived mostly at lunge distance, spending about 45 per cent of in-play time in the 1.5 to 2.6 m band where a lunge can score, with a further 35 per cent at advance-lunge range and only occasional excursions into infighting or out of distance. Both fencers spent a very similar share of their movement closing, so neither clearly imposed the ground game on the other. The scorer counts (five right, five left, four doubles) confirm a balanced exchange rather than one fencer dictating terms.

## Observed tendencies
- The distance profile is that of a bout fought at scoring range: about 45 per cent of in-play time at lunge distance and only 9 to 11 per cent either close or fully out of distance. The tactically live band dominated.
- Closing share sits at 56.3 per cent for fencer 1 and 56.6 per cent for fencer 2 in play. That difference is inside the noise margin, so read it as both fencers pressing forward to a very similar degree rather than one yielding.
- Touches came steadily, with a median gap of 15 s and a range of 5 to 20 s. Exchanges themselves had a median duration of about 10 s, so phrases tended to build before resolving rather than resolving on the first tempo.
- The tempo trend is essentially flat: the mean gap between touches lengthened by only 1.19 s from first half to second half. Any slowing was marginal, not a collapse in one fencer's rate.
- Scorer counts split five and five with four doubles, and the longest streak in the data is the run of doubles. That points to a bout where both fencers were arriving together often, which is worth reviewing.

## Suggestions to explore
- Review the four double touches together. A cluster of simultaneous arrivals at lunge distance often signals a timing or right-of-way habit worth drilling, so check whether both fencers were pulling the trigger on the same tempo.
- Since so much time was spent at lunge distance, look at how each fencer prepared their attacks from that band. Was the entry into distance disguised, or was it predictable enough that the opponent could arrive at the same moment?
- The longer exchanges (up to 19 s) are worth isolating in the video to see how phrases were being reset or broken, and whether either fencer had a reliable way to close them out.

## Data caveats
These figures come from a computer-vision prototype, so distances are height-normalised estimates rather than exact measurements. The touch count and timings are human confirmed and reliable, but the touch list does not describe who scored a given touch or how, so no touch here should be attributed to a fencer or read as a specific action. The net_displacement and net_forward_movement figures are flagged as implausible on this recording (magnitudes up to about 21.9 m where a metre would be expected after each reset), so they have not been used as evidence of aggression or territorial gain; the closing share figures, which count direction rather than distance, were used instead, and they are only accurate to a few percentage points. Total distance covered is deliberately not reported because it cannot be measured reliably from this data.
