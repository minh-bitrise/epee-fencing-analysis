## Bout summary
This bout was fought largely at long range, with the fencers spending most of the tracked time beyond 1.8m and only rarely closing to true infighting distance. The mean estimated distance of about 2.27m and the small fraction of time spent close together suggest a cautious, distance-managed contest rather than one built on repeated hard closes. Both fencers pushed and pulled in roughly equal measure, each ending with a slight net forward tendency. Fencer 2 covered noticeably more total ground in both directions, pointing to a more active or mobile fencer over the length of the video.

## Observed tendencies
- Time was dominated by far range, with 77.4 percent spent over 1.8m and only 0.6 percent under 1m, indicating both fencers preferred to hold distance rather than engage in close play.
- The engagement band from 1 to 1.8m accounted for 22.0 percent of tracked time, so most genuine trading opportunities likely occurred in a fairly narrow window.
- Fencer 2 recorded far more total movement (21.77m push and 19.6m pull) than Fencer 1 (11.82m and 10.36m), suggesting Fencer 2 was the busier mover across the video.
- Push share was near even for both fencers (53.3 percent and 52.6 percent), so neither showed a strong bias toward pressing forward versus giving ground.
- Net forward figures were modest for both (1.46m and 2.16m over the whole video), consistent with a lot of back-and-forth rather than sustained one-directional pressure.

## Data caveats
These metrics come from a computer-vision prototype, so distances are height-normalised estimates rather than precise measurements. All totals cover the whole video, including breaks between touches and walk-backs, because the system cannot yet detect when touches occur. There is no touch or score data here, so nothing above should be read as an account of points won or lost. Treat the figures as directional indicators to guide video review, not as exact in-play values.
