## Bout summary
This was a fairly measured epee bout of 190 seconds with 6 human-confirmed touches, an average of about 1.89 touches per minute. The fencers spent most of their time at extended range: roughly 51 per cent in the advance-lunge band and about 33 per cent out of distance, with only around 10 per cent at lunge distance and under 6 per cent in close infighting. In play, fencer 2 showed the slightly higher closing share (58.2 per cent versus 54.0 per cent for fencer 1), suggesting fencer 2 pressed forward somewhat more, though the gap is modest and both were above the halfway mark. The clearest signal in the data is the tempo: the bout quickened noticeably in its second half.

## Observed tendencies
- Over half the bout (51.3 per cent) was spent in the advance-lunge band and a further 32.9 per cent out of distance, so both fencers habitually held a range from which a step was needed before a lunge would land. The tactically live lunge band accounted for only 10.2 per cent of the time.
- The bout quickened markedly: the mean gap between touches fell from 46.0 s in the first half to 22.33 s in the second, a change of nearly 24 seconds. Something opened up in the later exchanges.
- Touch spacing was uneven (gaps ranging from 15 to 60 s, standard deviation 15.34 s), consistent with a bout that had both drawn-out probing phases and quicker back-to-back scoring.
- In-play closing shares were both above 50 per cent (54.0 and 58.2 per cent), with fencer 2 the marginally more forward of the two. Treat this as a slight lean rather than a firm tendency given the few-points margin.
- Of the 6 confirmed touches, 3 were doubles. The high proportion of simultaneous actions is worth noting, especially against the mostly long working distance.

## Suggestions to explore
- Review the second-half footage to see what changed as the tempo doubled: whether one fencer started forcing the distance, or whether fatigue and risk-taking shortened the exchanges for both.
- Look at the three double touches on video to understand what preparation or distance led to simultaneous arrivals, since these are frequent enough here to matter to the outcome.
- Given how much time was spent in the advance-lunge and out-of-distance bands, examine how each fencer managed the step into lunge distance, and whether the infrequent close-range moments were deliberate or accidental.

## Data caveats
These figures come from a computer-vision prototype and should be read as estimates. Distances are normalised approximations rather than precise measurements. Frame coverage was 73.7 per cent, so part of the action was not tracked. The movement figures derive from differenced cumulative totals that inherit a noise floor and movement cap, and the closing-share values are window-dependent, accurate only to a few percentage points, so small differences between the fencers should be treated as noise. The touch count is human-confirmed and reliable, and the scorer_counts label left, right and double totals, but this data does not tell us which fencer scored any individual touch or what action produced it, so no score, winner or streak has been inferred here. There is no measure of total distance covered, and none has been estimated.
