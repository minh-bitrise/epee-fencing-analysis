## Bout summary

This was a moderately active bout with an even distance profile. The fencers spent most of their time in the tactically live band, with around 41.5 per cent of the recording at lunge distance and a further 37.1 per cent at advance-lunge range, meaning offence usually required a preparatory step to land. Close infighting (9.8 per cent) and out-of-distance play (11.6 per cent) were both relatively rare. The mean gap between fencers was about 2.5 m in play. A tracker limitation on this recording means the per-fencer movement figures cannot be attributed to a specific fencer, so who pressed and who yielded cannot be stated here.

## Observed tendencies

- The approximate detector recorded 12 touches over three minutes, a rate of about 4.0 per minute. This is a fairly busy scoring pattern for epee.
- Touches were unevenly spaced: the median gap was 10.24 s but the range ran from 2.98 s to 49.42 s. The clustering early on (four touches inside the first 30 s) sits against a long quiet stretch late in the bout, with the final gap reaching about 49 s before the last touch at 171.5 s.
- The tempo trend points to a slowing bout: the mean gap between touches grew from 13.34 s in the first half to 16.57 s in the second, a change of about 3.2 s.
- Exchange durations were highly variable (median 7.31 s, max 43.42 s), suggesting a mix of quick single decisive actions and much longer, more searching phrases.
- Combined time at lunge and advance-lunge distance (roughly 79 per cent) indicates the bout was fought largely at or just outside scoring range rather than in prolonged infighting or from a safe standoff.

## Suggestions to explore

- Review the early cluster of touches (5.4 s to 30.0 s) against the slower second half to see whether one fencer imposed a faster rhythm at the start that then settled.
- Look at how the long quiet phrases developed, especially the near 49 s gap before the final touch, to understand what caused the search for openings to stall and whether a distance change could have forced an action sooner.
- With so much time spent in the advance-lunge band, examine on video who tended to initiate the step that brought the pair into scoring range, since the numbers here cannot answer that.

## Data caveats

The touches were proposed by an automatic detector, so the count of 12 is approximate rather than confirmed, and the source did not record who scored, so no score, winner, or momentum can be inferred. Distances are computer-vision estimates normalised by fencer height, not precise measurements. Critically, the tracker swapped which fencer each slot followed 15 times and the two came within 0.13 m in the measured position, so all per-fencer movement figures (net displacement, net forward movement, and closing share) are not attributable to either individual on this recording and should not be read as one fencer pressing or retreating. The closing-share figures are also window-dependent and accurate only to a few percentage points.
