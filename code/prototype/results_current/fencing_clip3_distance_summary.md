## Bout summary

This was a busy, well filled bout, with 14 human-confirmed touches over three minutes and roughly 57 per cent of the recording spent in live play. The fencers spent most of their time at lunge distance (45.2 per cent) with a further third of the time at advance-lunge range, so the working distance sat right at the edge of where a lunge can score. Both fencers pressed forward at very similar rates, with closing shares of about 52 per cent each, meaning ground was traded fairly evenly rather than one fencer dominating the piste. Touch spacing was fairly regular, and the tempo held roughly steady across the two halves.

## Observed tendencies

- Time was concentrated at lunge distance (45.2 per cent) and advance-lunge (34.7 per cent), with only 9.4 per cent close infighting and 10.8 per cent out of distance. The bout lived at the live striking edge rather than in either extreme.
- Closing shares were near identical (fencer 1 at 51.2 per cent, fencer 2 at 52.6 per cent in play). Both are within a few points of each other and of 50 per cent, so neither fencer pressed forward noticeably more than the other.
- Touch rate was high at 4.67 per minute, with a median gap of 15 seconds between touches and a median exchange duration of 10 seconds. Exchanges were substantial phrases rather than quick single actions.
- The tempo trend was essentially flat, with the mean gap moving from 11.67 seconds in the first half to 12.86 in the second, a change of about 1.2 seconds. Treat this as steady rather than a real slowing.
- Scorer counts were even at 5-5 with 4 doubles, and the longest streak recorded was the run of doubles. The high double count is worth noting as a feature of how the two attacked into each other.

## Suggestions to explore

- Review the four double touches on the video. With scoring split evenly and doubles this frequent, it is worth checking whether both fencers were committing into the same tempo and whether right of way or timing decisions could be cleaned up.
- Look at how phrases developed from lunge and advance-lunge distance, since that is where most of the bout sat. Consider whether either fencer could vary the preparation to break the even closing pattern.
- Check the short-gap sequences (the minimum gap was 5 seconds) against the longer 20 second gaps to see what changed in the setup between quick touches and slow ones.

## Data caveats

All distances are computer-vision estimates normalised by fencer height, not precise measurements, and pose-based tracking covered only 26.8 per cent of frames. The touch count is human confirmed and reliable for timing and spacing, but the timestamps say only when touches occurred, not who scored each one or what produced it, so the scorer split should not be read as momentum. The closing-share figures are approximate to a few percentage points because they depend on the smoothing window, so small differences between the fencers are noise. The net forward movement figures under in-play exclude the resets and are not a finishing position on the piste, so no one finished as far up the strip as those numbers might suggest. There is no reliable measure of total ground covered, and none has been given here.
