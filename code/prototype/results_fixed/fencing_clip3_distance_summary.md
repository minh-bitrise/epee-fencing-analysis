## Bout summary
This was an active, well-populated bout: 14 human-confirmed touches over 180 seconds, a rate of about 4.67 per minute. Distance sat mostly in the tactically live range, with roughly 45 per cent of time at lunge distance and another 35 per cent at advance-lunge distance, so the fencers spent the bulk of the bout within one action of scoring. Close infighting (about 9 per cent) and out-of-distance play (about 11 per cent) were both minority states. Closing share was near 52 per cent for both fencers, meaning ground was traded fairly evenly and neither fencer clearly dominated the forward pressure.

## Observed tendencies
- Distance discipline was tight: only about 11 per cent of time was spent out of distance, and the mean gap of roughly 2.4 m in play kept both fencers in or near scoring range for most exchanges.
- Closing share was 51.2 per cent for fencer 1 and 52.6 per cent for fencer 2 in play. That difference is within the noise of this measurement, so read it as an even split of who pressed forward rather than a tendency for either.
- The scorer counts were balanced at 5 right, 5 left and 4 doubles, with the longest run being 4 doubles. That is a high double rate (4 of 14 touches) and points to simultaneous or contested actions rather than one fencer imposing a clear pattern.
- Tempo was fairly steady with a slight slowing: first-half mean gap of 11.67 s versus 12.86 s in the second half, a change of about 1.2 s. The median gap of 15 s and median exchange of 10 s suggest a rhythm of moderate-length phrases separated by short pauses.
- Exchange durations varied widely (1 s to 19 s, standard deviation 5.4 s), so the bout mixed very quick single-tempo hits with longer, drawn-out phrases rather than settling into one style.

## Suggestions to explore
- Review the sequence of 4 doubles: worth watching the video to see whether these came from mutual attacks into distance or from failed defensive actions, since a quarter of the touches being doubles is tactically significant.
- Look at how the longer exchanges (near the 19 s maximum) resolved compared with the 1 s exchanges, to see which distance and which fencer initiative tended to produce the slow phrases.
- Given the high share of time at advance-lunge distance (about 35 per cent), check whether preparation from that band was being converted or whether it mostly reset without a scoring attempt.

## Data caveats
These figures come from a computer-vision prototype. Distances are height-normalised estimates rather than precise measurements, and closing share is window-dependent and accurate only to a few percentage points, so small gaps between the fencers should be treated as noise. Touch counts are human-confirmed and reliable in number, but the data does not tell us who produced each touch through what action, and the scorer counts should not be turned into a score or a winner. Net forward movement in play is not a finishing position, and no figure here measures total ground covered.
